# How to draw a benchtop plan

Each job has one benchtop plan: the stone pieces, drawn to scale, ready to print for the stone supplier.

## Set up the lists (admins, once)

Admin > **Benchtop lists**. Add each material (supplier, range, colour, thickness in mm) and check the edge finishes (code, name, line style). Untick **Shown** to hide one from new choices; plans that already use it keep it.

## Start a plan

Open the job, go to its **Benchtops** section and click **Benchtop plan**. Existing plans are also listed on Projects > Benchtops.

## Add pieces

Click **Straight**, **L shape (left)**, **L shape (right)** or **U**. The plan opens on **All**, which draws every piece together to scale. Drag a piece to arrange it (for example the island in front of the run); the arrangement is saved and printed. Click a piece in the list to select it; click it again to see it on its own, or **All** to go back. The red × on the selected piece deletes it (you are asked first).

- **Sizes:** click any size on the drawing, type the new length in mm and press Enter (Esc cancels). Depths are the end edges' sizes, typed the same way. New runs start 600 deep.
- **Material:** on the right, pick supplier and range, colour, thickness.

## Edges, joins and cut-outs

The plan follows the usual stone-drawing convention: every edge is a thin unbroken line, and a mark repeated across it says how it is finished.

- **X** unfinished, against a wall or panel. **//** finished and seen (arris, pencil round, bullnose). **[]** seen and finished square.
- **Edges:** click an edge, then choose its finish on the right.
- **Thicker edges:** if an edge is built up (say 40 mm on a 20 mm top), type it under Edge thickness. It draws bold, and the legend says what bold means.
- **Waterfall:** on an end edge, choose **Waterfall** and type the drop height (mm) in the box that opens. It shows as "WF 870".
- **Joins:** click **Place join**, then click along a run (drawn dashed). Adjust the position on the right.
- **Cut-outs:** select a piece and click **Add cut-out**, then choose what it is (sink, cooktop, tap hole, other). Cut-outs are drawn dashed and labelled; their sizes are not shown. If a cut-out's position matters, tick **Show location**: it is dimensioned from the piece's one reference edge (marked REF). Change the reference edge under the piece, or with **Make this the reference edge** on a selected edge.

## Rounded corners

Right-click an outside corner on the drawing (on a tablet, press and hold) and choose **Radius…**, then type the radius in mm and press Save (or Enter). **Remove radius** squares it again. It is drawn rounded with a leader to "R100", on screen and on the plan. Inside corners of an L or U can't be rounded. The limit is half the shorter of the two edges meeting at the corner. Lengths are still measured to the square corner, and the area counts the square outline.

## Totals

The right panel (and the print) shows the total area in m² (and per material when there is more than one) and the metres of each edge finish and of any thicker edges. Area counts the whole outline: joins and cut-outs don't reduce it. Lengths are added up exactly, then area is rounded to 2 decimals and metres to 1 decimal.

Type notes for the supplier on the left, then **Save**.

## Print

Pieces are lettered A, B, C... in the order of the pieces list, on screen and on paper.



**Print plan set** saves, then opens the plan set on A4 landscape. Every sheet has a slim header (letterhead, job and revision, and on the right the totals in bold with the material swatches and edge metres); the drawing takes the full width, at the largest scale that fits (1:30 or closer when it can); underneath, a short band gives the key, the pieces, the cut-outs and the notes. Long notes carry on to a second sheet with the same header. Each piece is filled with a pale tint for its material (light grey when there is only one).

**Send to supplier** is coming later.
